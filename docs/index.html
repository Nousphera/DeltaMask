DeltaMask: Minimizing Communication in Federated Fine-Tuning of Vision Foundation Models

Adapt foundation models by communicating sparse mask changes.

Vasileios Tsouvalas · Yuki M. Asano · Aaqib Saeed

At a glance

Adapt a frozen model by communicating mask changes. DeltaMask trains stochastic masks over pretrained weights, selects changes to a shared reference mask, and encodes their positions with probabilistic filters. The server approximately reconstructs those masks and updates global mask probabilities.

What this adds

  • 01

    Mask-based adaptation: learn subnetworks while keeping pretrained backbone weights fixed.

  • 02

    Sparse change encoding: rank differing positions and compress them with a binary fuse filter.

  • 03

    Controllable trade-off: vary retained changes and fingerprint width to balance bitrate and accuracy.

Abstract

Foundation Models (FMs) have revolutionized machine learning with their adaptability and high performance across tasks; yet, their integration into Federated Learning (FL) is challenging due to substantial communication overhead from their extensive parameterization. Current communication-efficient FL strategies, such as gradient compression, reduce bitrates to around 1 bit-per-parameter (bpp). However, these approaches fail to harness the characteristics of FMs, with their large number of parameters still posing a challenge to communication efficiency, even at these bitrate regimes. In this work, we present DeltaMask, a novel method that efficiently fine-tunes FMs in FL at an ultra-low bitrate, well below 1 bpp. DeltaMask employs stochastic masking to detect highly effective subnetworks within FMs and leverage stochasticity and sparsity in client masks to compress updates into a compact grayscale image using probabilistic filters, deviating from traditional weight training approaches. Our comprehensive evaluations across various datasets and architectures demonstrate DeltaMask efficiently achieves bitrates as low as 0.09 bpp, enhancing communication efficiency while maintaining FMs performance, as measured on 8 datasets and 5 pre-trained models of various network architectures.

How it works

DeltaMask

01

Optimize a stochastic mask

Transform unconstrained mask scores with a sigmoid, sample binary activations, and apply them to fixed pretrained weights. The classifier first receives one round of linear probing.

θ=sigmoid(s),m∼Bern(θ),w′=m⊙winit

Sections III-A–C and Algorithm 1, PDF pages 4–6. The experiments target the last five backbone blocks.

02

Form and rank mask differences

Sample the global reference with a shared seed. Collect positions where local and reference bits disagree, rank them by relative entropy of their probabilities, and retain a top-κ subset.

Δ={i:mlocal,i≠mref,i}

Section III-B and Equation 4, PDF page 5, with simplified round/client notation. The main setup schedules κ from 0.8.

03

Filter first, compress bytes second

Encode selected positions in a four-wise binary fuse filter using 8-bit fingerprints by default. PNG-like DEFLATE compression then shrinks the fingerprint bytes losslessly.

Section III-B, PDF page 5. Selection and probabilistic membership make mask recovery approximate; DEFLATE itself does not discard fingerprint data.

04

Recover masks and update probabilities

Query membership for every possible position, flip the corresponding reference bits, and feed reconstructed client masks to Bayesian aggregation for the next round.

m^=mref⊕𝟙Δ′^

Equation 5 and Algorithm 1, PDF pages 5–6. False positives can flip extra bits; this is not a lossless-recovery or formal privacy guarantee.

Takeaways

  • Send changes to a shared mask. The small update set is the key resource exploited by the filter.
  • Low bitrate still has an accuracy cost. The selected eight-task panels report 0.151 and 0.233 bpp, with accuracy below their fine-tuning references.
  • Approximation is explicit. Ranking and filter false positives affect recovery; lossless byte compression does not make the whole pipeline lossless.
Study scope & sources
  • Accepted IEEE BigData manuscript; image classification across eight datasets and five pretrained backbones.
  • The 0.09 bpp headline is a lowest reported setting, not the mean across tasks; fine-tuning targets the same last five blocks.
Data
CIFAR-10/100, SVHN, EMNIST, Fashion-MNIST, EuroSAT, Food-101 and Cars196; image classification only.
Models
CLIP ViT-B/32 and ViT-L/14, DINOv2-Base and DINOv2-Small, and ConvMixer-768/32; masks target the last five blocks.
Compared with
Linear probing, fine-tuning, FedMask, EDEN, DeepReduce and FedPM; additional CPU/traffic comparisons include DRIVE and FedCode.
Protocol
Shared seeded reference masks; one initial linear-probing round for classifier initialization; BFuse8 by default, with ranking and filter-bitwidth ablations.

Scope & limitations

  • The experiments cover image classification. Broader task support and personalization are proposals rather than evaluated outcomes.
  • The 0.09 bpp headline is a lowest reported setting; Tables II–III give eight-task average bitrates of 0.151–0.233 bpp for DeltaMask.
  • The CPU encoding/decoding study excludes aggregation and omits arithmetic coding for masking baselines; it is not an end-to-end device latency test.
  • Probabilistic filters permit false positives. Hashing and bit flips are not evaluated as a formal privacy mechanism.
