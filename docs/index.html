DeltaMask: Minimizing Communication in Federated Fine-Tuning of Vision Foundation Models

Adapt foundation models by communicating sparse mask changes.

Vasileios Tsouvalas · Yuki M. Asano · Aaqib Saeed

PaperCodePoster
OverviewMethodResultsCitation
The challenge

Even one-bit updates become expensive when many clients adapt large pretrained vision models.

The contribution

DeltaMask trains stochastic masks over fixed pretrained weights and encodes selected changes from a shared mask using compact probabilistic filters.

Method overview · Based on the paper

The idea

At a glance

Large vision models make federated updates costly, even when compressed to one bit per parameter. DeltaMask keeps pre-trained weights fixed and learns stochastic masks, then communicates selected changes through compact probabilistic filters so clients can collaborate at a fraction of the bitrate of a full binary mask.

  • Sparse changes to a shared mask can be much smaller than sending the complete binary mask.
  • The practical result is a bitrate–accuracy trade-off, not uniformly unchanged accuracy or a universal 0.09 bpp rate.
  • Federated fine-tuning
  • Stochastic masking
  • Communication compression
Read the abstract

Foundation Models (FMs) have revolutionized machine learning with their adaptability and high performance across tasks; yet, their integration into Federated Learning (FL) is challenging due to substantial communication overhead from their extensive parameterization. Current communication-efficient FL strategies, such as gradient compression, reduce bitrates to around 1 bit-per-parameter (bpp). However, these approaches fail to harness the characteristics of FMs, with their large number of parameters still posing a challenge to communication efficiency, even at these bitrate regimes. In this work, we present DeltaMask, a novel method that efficiently fine-tunes FMs in FL at an ultra-low bitrate, well below 1 bpp. DeltaMask employs stochastic masking to detect highly effective subnetworks within FMs and leverage stochasticity and sparsity in client masks to compress updates into a compact grayscale image using probabilistic filters, deviating from traditional weight training approaches. Our comprehensive evaluations across various datasets and architectures demonstrate DeltaMask efficiently achieves bitrates as low as 0.09 bpp, enhancing communication efficiency while maintaining FMs performance, as measured on 8 datasets and 5 pre-trained models of various network architectures.

The approach

How it works

  1. 01

    Learn a stochastic subnetwork

    Hold pre-trained backbone weights fixed and optimize mask probabilities using local data. Sample binary masks to select which weights participate in the downstream task.

  2. 02

    Encode the important differences

    Compare the local mask with the shared server mask. Rank changed positions by relative entropy, retain selected updates and store their fingerprints in a binary fuse filter compressed as a grayscale image.

  3. 03

    Reconstruct and aggregate masks

    The server uses filter membership queries to reconstruct client masks, then updates global mask probabilities through Bayesian aggregation for the next round.

Experiments & evidence

What the results show

Read accuracy together with bitrate: the tabulated averages are not the same as the paper’s lowest reported 0.09-bit setting.

Data
CIFAR-10/100, SVHN, EMNIST, Fashion-MNIST, EuroSAT, Food-101 and Cars196; image classification only.
Models
CLIP ViT-B/32 and ViT-L/14, DINOv2-Base and DINOv2-Small, and ConvMixer-768/32; masks target the last five blocks.
Compared with
Linear probing, fine-tuning, FedMask, EDEN, DeepReduce and FedPM; additional CPU/traffic comparisons include DRIVE and FedCode.
Protocol
Shared seeded reference masks; one initial linear-probing round for classifier initialization; BFuse8 by default, with ranking and filter-bitwidth ablations.

Scope & limitations

  • The experiments cover image classification. Broader task support and personalization are proposals rather than evaluated outcomes.
  • The 0.09 bpp headline is a lowest reported setting; Tables II–III give eight-task average bitrates of 0.151–0.233 bpp for DeltaMask.
  • The CPU encoding/decoding study excludes aggregation and omits arithmetic coding for masking baselines; it is not an end-to-end device latency test.
  • Probabilistic filters permit false positives. Hashing and bit flips are not evaluated as a formal privacy mechanism.
